## Two routes, one response

| Route | Model path |
|---|---|
| **Through the virus** | Heat acts through viral titer before reaching the gene response |
| **Direct** | Heat acts directly on the gene response |

<details>
  <summary><strong>How the split is calculated</strong></summary>

  The analysis fits `M ~ X` and `Y ~ X + M`. The indirect effect is $ab$; the direct effect is $c'$. Their ratio gives the mediated share.

  I used 2,000 bootstrap resamples to estimate uncertainty.
</details>

## Vineyard result

- **MBF1c:** 61.8% through virus · 38.2% direct
- **Gene screen:** 39 mediators / 2,000 genes

<Takeaway>
61.8% describes MBF1c—not every heat response in the vineyard.
</Takeaway>

## Same method, new scale

| Role in the model | Grapevine study | Farming re-application |
|---|---|---|
| Starting variable | Seasonal temperature | Year |
| Possible go-between | Viral titer | Harvested area |
| Measured outcome | Gene expression | Crop production |

| Signal | Crops |
|---|---|
| Land route exceeds total | Soybean · Tomato |

<Takeaway>
Values outside 0–100% are warnings, not tidy shares.
</Takeaway>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Limits of the comparison</strong></summary>

  The crop analysis separates observational trends; it does not prove that land change caused production change. Breeding, irrigation, policy, trade, and region also changed over time. Mediation shows how a modeled effect divides—it cannot choose the causal story by itself.
</details>
